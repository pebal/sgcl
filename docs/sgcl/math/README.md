# sgcl::math

Numbers that the language does not have and randomness that the standard library gives in a shape nobody wants: whole numbers of any size and fractions of them, and a generator with the questions a program asks of one. `#include "sgcl/math/math.h"` brings the module in; it depends on [`core`](../core/README.md) and [`txt`](../txt/README.md) (for [`format`](../txt/format.md)) and on nothing else — never on `concurrent`, `async` or [`io`](../io/README.md) — so that `encoding` (ASN.1) and `crypto` (the public keys of X.509) can be built on it. The index of the whole interface is [`docs/sgcl/`](../README.md).

| | |
|---|---|
| [big_integer](big_integer.md) | a whole number of any size, with the manners of an `int`: `a * 2 + 1`, `a / b` cut towards zero and `a % b` with the sign of the dividend, `mod` always in `[0, \|m\|)`, `div_rem`, the bits in two's complement stretching without end to the left, shifts, comparisons with the language's own integers at no cost, text in the bases 2 to 36 both ways, bytes, `to_double` rounded as a conversion of an `int64_t` is, the literal `_big`, `txt::format` and `std::hash`; `pow`, `sqrt`, `gcd`, `lcm`, `mod_pow`, `mod_inverse`, `is_probable_prime`, `factorial`, `binomial`; sixteen bytes, a value within `int64_t` allocating nothing |
| [rational](rational.md) | a fraction of two `big_integer`s, always in lowest terms: exact arithmetic with the operators, order, `abs`, `inverse`, `pow` with a negative exponent, `floor`, `ceil`; `parse` of `"3/4"` and of decimals with an exponent, `rational(double)` exactly, `to_double` rounded once, `to_decimal` half away from zero, `txt::format` with `{:.5f}`, `std::hash` |
| [random](random.md) | a generator — ChaCha8Rand, the stream of Go's `ChaCha8` from the same key — and what is asked of it: `next_int(n)`, `next_int(first, last)`, `next_uint64`, `next_double`, `next_bool`, `next_normal`, `next_exponential`, `next_bytes`, `shuffle`, `pick`, `permutation`, and a uniform random bit generator of the standard's for everything else |

## The rules of the module

**A number is a value.** `big_integer` behaves as `int` does: operators, conversions from any whole number, comparisons, a copy that is a copy. Under it is what [`string`](../core/string.md) is — an immutable object on the managed heap, shared by copying a word — and a value that fits in `int64_t` is not on the heap at all. A copy never changes with the value it was taken from: `a += b` means `a = a + b`, and the old value is still whole for whoever else holds it; only a value nobody else has — the result of an operation not copied since — is written in place by `+=`, `-=` and `*=` by a small value, which is where Go reuses its receiver. A `big_integer` holds a `tracked_ptr`, so it lives where one may: on a stack, in a managed object, in a container of the library.

**An error of the program throws, an error of data does not.** A division by zero, a negative shift, a NaN made into a number, a base outside 2 to 36, a bound of zero for `next_int` are mistakes of the code that asked, and throw (`domain_error`, `invalid_argument`, `length_error`, `out_of_range`) where Go panics and `int` would be undefined. Text that is not a number is a fact about the text and comes back as an [`expected`](../core/expected.md) whose error says where the reading stopped (`offset()`) and why (`message()`).

**One road to each thing.** `/` and `%` are C++'s; the one other division modular arithmetic wants is `mod`. There is one generator and no function of a hidden global one: make a `random` and ask it.

**Not for secrets.** Nothing in `big_integer` takes the same time whatever the value — the length of a number, its fast paths and the corrections of the division all show — and a `random` is keyed from a number or copied at will. A private key, a signature and the choosing of a prime for a key belong to `crypto`, which has types of its own for them; `big_integer` may carry what is public (a modulus, a serial number, an INTEGER of DER).

**Its own implementation, from the specification.** The algorithms are written from Knuth (TAOCP vol. 2: 4.3.1 for the division, 4.3.3 for Karatsuba and Toom, 4.5.2 for Lehmer's gcd), Möller and Granlund (division by an invariant integer), Bodrato and Zanoni (the interpolation of Toom-3), Burnikel and Ziegler and Brent and Zimmermann's Modern Computer Arithmetic (the recursive division and the conversions), Montgomery (multiplication without division), Baillie and Wagstaff (the Lucas test), Marsaglia and Tsang (the ziggurat method) and the C2SP specification of ChaCha8Rand. Python's `int`, Go's `math/big` and Go's `math/rand/v2` are oracles in the tests and nowhere else.

## SGCL and Go

| | Go | SGCL |
|---|---|---|
| a whole number | `big.Int`, a pointer, the result into a receiver: `z.Add(x, y)` | `big_integer`, a value: `a + b`, `a += b` |
| small values | a slice of words, allocated | inside the value, nothing allocated |
| a copy | `new(big.Int).Set(x)`, the words copied | two words, the object shared and never changed |
| division | `Quo`/`Rem` cutting towards zero, `Div`/`Mod` Euclidean | `/` `%` as `int`, `mod()` never negative, `div_rem()` both at once |
| a constant in an expression | `big.NewInt(2)` every time | `a * 2 + 1` |
| a literal | none (`SetString` when the program runs) | `0xffff'ffff'ffff'ffff'ffff_big`, the digits checked by the compiler |
| text | `Text(base)` 2 to 62, `SetString` whose base 0 guesses a prefix | `to_string(base)` 2 to 36, `parse` into an `expected`, prefixes only in the literal |
| formatting | `fmt` with `%x %b %o %d` | `txt::format("{:#x}", a)`, width, fill and sign as for any number, the specification checked by the compiler |
| errors | a panic on a division by zero; `nil, false` from `SetString` | `domain_error`; an `expected` with `offset()` and `message()` |
| the bits of a negative number | two's complement, infinitely wide | the same |
| a random generator | `rand.IntN` on a global source and `rand.New(src).IntN` on one's own | one type: `math::random r; r.next_int(n)` |
| its algorithm | `ChaCha8`, `PCG` | ChaCha8Rand only, the same stream as Go's from the same key |
| a range | `IntN(n)` | `next_int(n)` and `next_int(first, last)`, half-open like [`range`](../core/range.md) |
| from a seed | `rand.NewChaCha8([32]byte{...})` | `math::random(42)`: Go's stream for the key of 42 in eight bytes little-endian and 24 zeros; `next_int`, `next_double`, `shuffle` and `permutation` answer what Go's `Int64N`, `Float64`, `Shuffle` and `Perm` answer from it |
| a shuffle | `Shuffle(n, swap)` | `shuffle(v)` over any range of random access, `pick(v)` |
| a fraction | `big.Rat`, a pointer, the result into a receiver; `SetString`, `FloatString`, `Float64` | `rational`, a value: `third * 2 + 1`, `r < 1`; `parse`, `to_decimal`, `to_double`, `{:.5f}`; a sum takes the common factors out as Knuth does rather than reducing the whole product |
| number theory | `Exp` (a negative exponent an inverse), `GCD` with cofactors, `ModInverse`, `Sqrt`, `ProbablyPrime(n)`, `MulRange`, `Binomial` | `mod_pow`, `gcd`, `lcm`, `mod_inverse` into an `optional`, `sqrt`, `pow`, `is_probable_prime(rounds)` (Baillie–PSW and `rounds` bases seeded by the number: one answer in every run), `factorial`, `binomial` |
| distributions | `NormFloat64`, `ExpFloat64` | `next_normal`, `next_exponential`, ziggurats over tables written out as literals, so a seed gives the same numbers on every platform but for a rare draw through `std::exp` or `std::log`; and any `std::*_distribution` through `operator()` |
| for secrets | neither `math/big` nor `math/rand` | neither: `crypto` |

## How it is tested

`tests_math`, one program; `-R math` or the target `tests_math` alone. The oracle for `big_integer` is Python's `int`: `tools/math_vectors.py` writes `tests/math/vectors.h`, every operation over every pair of named operands — the edges of `int64_t` and one either side, `2^64k` and one either side, limbs of all ones and of zeros, all four combinations of signs — and over random operands of 1 to 40 limbs; divisions that take the rare add-back step of Knuth's algorithm, each confirmed to take it by a model of the algorithm in the generator; the ties of `to_double`; text in every base. The fast algorithms have oracles of two kinds: `tools/math_vectors.py fast` writes `tests/math/fast_vectors.h`, the answers of Python for products, squares and divisions of up to a hundred thousand limbs and for conversions of up to a million digits, whose operands both sides make from the same seeds; and the schoolbook algorithms themselves, which every fast road must agree with when the thresholds are lowered to a few limbs, so that each is taken on small numbers, and at the thresholds as shipped. The number theory is checked against Python's `math` (`tools/math_vectors.py number`: `pow`, `isqrt`, `gcd`, `lcm`, `pow(a, e, m)`, `pow(a, -1, m)`, `factorial`, `comb`) and `is_probable_prime` against Go's `ProbablyPrime` (`tools/math_primes_oracle.go`), against a sieve below 2^16, and against the pseudoprimes of the literature: Carmichael numbers, strong pseudoprimes to base 2 and to every prime base up to 41, the squares of the Wieferich primes, strong Lucas pseudoprimes — each half of Baillie–PSW shown to pass the other's liars and to fail its own. `rational` is checked against Python's `fractions.Fraction` and `float` (`tools/math_vectors.py rational`): the four operations and the order over pairs of named and random fractions, `floor` and `ceil`, the double nearest a fraction at the ties of the normal and the subnormal range and at the edges, the fraction a double is, parsing; `to_decimal` against the definition of rounding half away from zero. The laws of arithmetic are checked over thousands of random operands besides, and `big_integer` is exercised under a collector running in a loop, with a count of the pages left when the numbers are gone. For `random` the first test is the C2SP specification's own sample output; `tools/math_oracle.go` writes `tests/math/random_vectors.h` from Go's `math/rand/v2`; the distributions are held to their shapes by chi-square and Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests on fixed seeds, and to the values `random(42)` gave when they were written down.

## What is still to come

The three types of the sketch are in: `big_integer` with its fast algorithms and its number theory, `rational`, `random`. A result nobody else has is written in place by `+=`, `-=` and `*=` by a small value (the question of the sketch, answered by measurement: `sum += x` over a hundred limbs 109 ns a step before, 37 after, Go 35). The loops over limbs stayed without assembler and came out ahead of Go's with it at every length but `gcd`'s. Open: a multiplication by number-theoretic transforms for numbers of millions of digits. The numbers against Go are measured on a quiet machine: `bench_math` and `benchmarks/go/math` hold the same cases under the same names.
